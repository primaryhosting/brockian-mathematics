The four load-bearing claims
A

AI proposes, the checker certifies. Models draft specs, SAWScript, lemmas and tactics and re-prove under churn — inside the loop, outside the trusted base. Acceleration never silently becomes trust.

B

Trust is relocated, not re-asserted. Heterogeneous provers must independently agree — the one assurance axis a single toolchain cannot self-audit. Additive to SAW, not redundant.

C

The certificate is the product. Signed, portable, third-party re-checkable without rerunning the pipeline — composing with DRAT, Alethe/Carcara and LFSC, not replacing them.

D

A machine-enforced honesty taxonomy. Every artifact carries a checked grade; a silent upgrade is a compile-time error. The “verified component vs. verified system” line becomes a tracked state, not a footnote.

We raise the objections first

The honest limits — every one a Galois reviewer would raise, raised by us.

Agreement is not independence. Provers can share an SMT-LIB frontend or bit-blasting core, so bugs can correlate. The honest claim is “not the same bug,” never a soundness proof.
The spec stays trusted. Cross-prover agreement does nothing about a wrong hand-written Cryptol spec. Putting AI on the toil changes labor cost, not the trust boundary.
The crypto / seL4 / capDL framing is a proposed port, not a delivered result. The concrete assets today are a Z3 data-isolation engine (on application data models) and a Lean/AXLE cross-verifier from our math work — not an seL4 deliverable. Said out loud, unprompted.
Demo-or-die, in a different domain. The proof-of-life is a real obligation cleared by two independent provers with a re-checkable certificate — ideally a known HMAC/SHA equivalence in SAW. Until then it is a concept, not an asset.
No numbers yet. No throughput, coverage, or “parses capDL” claim appears until a reproducible artifact backs it. A KPI without a re-checkable artifact reads as hype to this audience.
“We could build this ourselves.” Galois can build a gate in a week. The argument leans only on the genuinely additive piece — cross-toolchain soundness reduction it cannot self-audit from inside one prover stack.

## Slot-in B — "Interlude: Primes in Space" (Part V, between Ch. 9 and Ch. 10,
or folded as §10.0)

**Placement rationale:** Ch. 9 gives the honest genesis of the figure (Philosophy of
Arithmetic → the 2007 construction → the Brockian map); Ch. 10 gives the foundations
crisis as a dismembered constitutive cycle with Weyl as "the student in both schools."
This interlude is the hinge between them: what the figure *does* phenomenologically —
an instrument of givenness — and why givenness certifies nothing, told through
Cantor–Dedekind (the straight line as adopted axiom), Husserl 1891 (number as given),
Frege 1894 (the review the book already discusses in §9.1 — wire the cross-reference),
and Weyl 1918 (already the hero of Ch. 10 — this interlude sets up his entrance).

**Substitutions for the book:**
- The site version's line "machine-verified in Lean" stays, but footnote it to
  Interlude 2.7 (Slot-in A) so the two insertions carry each other.

  review of the legacy manuscripts (same documentary method as §14.5's "Advice, Read
  Nineteen Years Late" — a second audit of the project's own past, and it belongs in
  that lineage; consider a one-line cross-reference there).
- The closing sentence ("let perception guide the conjecture, let the machine keep the
  books, and publish the difference") lands adjacent to Ch. 10's thesis that genesis and
  sedimentation are moments of one cycle — add the bridge line: "The cycle Ch. 10
  reassembles is walked here at the smallest possible scale: one figure, one seeing,
  one proof, one register."

**Registers:** the Cantor–Dedekind axiom [established, standard]; Husserl/Frege/Weyl
history [documented]; "instrument of givenness" [interpretation, the book's own
